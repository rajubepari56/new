**Key takeaways**

- Clicks are not leads. Before changing ads, check that your tracking counts leads correctly.
- Most "no lead" problems come from four places: the search terms, the landing page, the tracking and how fast you respond.
- Fix them in that order. Each one is quick to check.
</div>

## 1. Check the tracking first

If the account says zero conversions, the leads may exist but not be counted. Submit your own form and call your own tracking number, then see whether Google Ads records them. A broken form tag, a thank-you page that never loads or a call number that is not tracked is more common than people expect. My [conversion tracking checklist](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-checklist/) walks through it.

## 2. Read the search terms, not the keywords

Open the search terms report. You are looking for searches that show the wrong intent: people looking for jobs, free advice, DIY help or something you do not sell. If much of the spend goes there, add negative keywords and tighten match types.

## 3. Match the ad to the page

If the ad promises "same-day quotes" and the page opens with a long company history, visitors leave. The page should repeat the promise in its first screen, show one clear action and work well on a phone.

## 4. Look at the device and the clock

Check results by device and by hour. Many local businesses lose calls because ads run when nobody answers. An ad schedule that follows your opening hours often helps.

## 5. Check the location settings

"People in or regularly in your target locations" is safer than "interested in" for most local businesses. Otherwise you can pay for clicks from people who cannot use your service.

## 6. Look at how you respond

A lead that waits a day for a reply is often gone. If calls go to voicemail or forms land in a crowded inbox, the ads are not the problem.

## 7. Check the offer and the price of a lead

Sometimes the traffic is right and the offer is weak. Compare your cost per lead with what a customer is worth to you. If a lead costs more than you can earn from it, change the offer or the target, not just the bid.
