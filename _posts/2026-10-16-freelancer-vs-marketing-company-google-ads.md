**Key takeaways**

- A freelancer is best when you want direct access to the person doing the work.
- A larger company can suit you if you need many services, such as design, video and print, under one roof.
- Ask who will actually log into your account. That answer matters more than the label.
</div>

## Who does the work?

With a freelance specialist, the person you speak to is the person in your account. With a larger company, you often speak to a sales person or account manager, and someone else does the work. That can be fine if the team is strong, but ask who it is.

## Communication

A direct line means fewer messages passed along and fewer misunderstandings. It also means one person is accountable for the numbers. The tradeoff is that a freelancer has limited hours, so ask how many clients they handle and how fast they reply.

## Cost

Pricing varies widely on both sides, so compare what is included, not just the figure. A larger company has more overhead to cover. A freelancer has less, but will not offer every service. Ask whether ad spend is paid directly to Google (it should be) and whether you own the accounts.

## Range of services

If you need ads, a full website rebuild, video and branding at once, a larger company may be simpler. If your main problem is ads, tracking and analytics, a specialist can go deeper.

## Risk

A freelancer can be unavailable if they get sick or leave, so ask about a backup plan and insist on documentation. A larger company has more people, but your account may still depend on one junior person.

## Questions to ask either one

1. Who will work in my account day to day?
2. Will my accounts stay in my name, with admin access for me?
3. How do you track leads and sales, and can you show me?
4. What will you report, and how often?
5. What happens if results are poor after a few months?
