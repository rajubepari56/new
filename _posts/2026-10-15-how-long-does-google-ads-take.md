## Clicks are fast. Results are slower.

Once an ad is approved, it can show within hours. That does not mean it performs well yet. Early weeks are used to learn which searches, ads and pages bring real customers.

## Why the learning period exists

Automated bidding adjusts bids using past conversions. With few conversions it has little to learn from, and results can swing from day to day. Changing the strategy too often resets that learning, which is why I avoid constant tweaks in the first weeks.

## A realistic way to think about it

- **First days:** the account is live, I check that tracking counts every lead and I remove obvious waste in the search terms.
- **First weeks:** enough data collects to see which keywords, ads and pages work.
- **After that:** I shift budget toward what brings customers and keep testing.

Your timeline depends on your budget, how often customers search and how many conversions you get. A business with a few leads a month learns more slowly than one with hundreds.

## What speeds it up

1. **Correct tracking from day one.** Wrong data teaches the system the wrong lesson. See [GA4 and Google Ads numbers that do not match](/blog/google-ads-vs-ga4-numbers-dont-match/).
2. **A focused structure.** Fewer, clearer campaigns collect data faster.
3. **Real sales data.** Sending closed sales back to Google Ads helps bidding find better customers. Here is [how to send CRM sales back to Google Ads](/blog/send-crm-sales-back-to-google-ads/).
